Two features. No SQL — both use what the database already has.

1. FLOATING CALCULATOR, TWO MODES
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
A round button in the corner opens a panel; Escape or ✕ closes it. Mounted in
Layout, so it follows you across pages and keeps whatever you were part-way
through typing — per page it would reset on every navigation.

BASIC: the four operators, brackets, a keypad, and history.

FORMULA: "students * costPerStudent". Type a name into the formula and its
input appears — there is no separate "add a variable" step to forget. Names,
values and the formula all persist, and Hebrew names work.

Both modes run through the SAME evaluator, so precedence cannot differ between
them and there is one place for a bug rather than two.

TWO DECISIONS WORTH FLAGGING

  Not eval or new Function. Both would execute whatever is typed, with access
  to everything the page can reach — and these strings are SAVED and restored,
  so a value from a stale localStorage entry would run on load. It is a
  tokeniser and a shunting-yard pass instead: numbers, names, + - * / ( ) and
  no syntax for reaching anything. `window.location` evaluates to "no value for
  window", not to an object.

  localStorage, not cookies. You asked for cookies, but they cap near 4 kB for
  the whole domain and are attached to EVERY request the page makes — including
  every Supabase query. A growing history would be uploaded on each one, for no
  benefit, since nothing on the server reads it. localStorage gives ~5 MB, is
  never transmitted, and is already how the simulation scenario persists.

Tested against hostile input: unbalanced brackets, "1.2.3", "2 ** 3", division
by zero, and a missing variable each produce a named error rather than a wrong
number.

2. START VS END OF YEAR
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
On the dashboard and in the simulation:

    opening  →  today  →  − still committed  =  projected year end

The projection is your formula: today's balance less everything budgeted and
not yet spent. An approved budget nobody has touched is money already promised,
and counting only what has LEFT the account makes a fully-budgeted season look
healthy right until the invoices arrive.

It agrees with the funding-gap figure the budgets page already shows —
₪-128,241.65 on your data — which is asserted in the audit, because two
different numbers for the same thing would be worse than neither.

IN THE SIMULATION it also carries the planned income and spend. That is the
case you said mattered, and it is the only place the effect of simulated
sponsorship on the year-end figure appears at all — every other figure on that
page stops at today's balance.

Two cases that could have quietly lied, both asserted:
  · an OVERSPENT budget has no remaining commitment — otherwise going over
    would credit the projection
  · an UNDERSPENT budget stays committed — money not spent was never received,
    so an underspend is not income

The year-end figure is on the shared PDF too. "What is left today" and "where
the season lands" are different answers, and the second is the one a reader
acts on.
